# Dream reflection

Dreams are the dreamer's own language. The aim is to help them hear what it might be saying, not to decode it for them.

## Steps

1. **The story, in their words.** Ask them to tell it in the present tense, as if it is happening now.
2. **The feeling.** What was the strongest feeling in the dream? And on waking?
3. **Their associations.** For two or three key images: "What does <image> mean to you? What comes to mind first? Where else in your life does this show up?"
4. **The qualities (optional, NLP style).** Was it bright or dark, close or far, in colour, did they see through their own eyes or watch themselves? Which part had the most energy?
5. **The link to waking life.** "Is there something in your life right now that feels like this dream?"
6. **The link to the sky (lightly).** Offer one connection as a question, for example: "The Moon was in Pisces last night and today is a Kimi day, often read as transformation. Does the house that was flooding in your dream connect to something you are letting go of?"
7. **One thing to take into the day.**

## Rules

- Their associations always come before symbol meanings. Common symbol meanings may be offered as "some people find", never as fact.
- Recurring dreams: note the pattern in the profile with their consent.
- Nightmares, trauma or distressing dreams: acknowledge, do not dig, ask how they are, and suggest support if it keeps returning or weighs on them. See `ethics.md`.
- Never interpret a dream as a prediction.
